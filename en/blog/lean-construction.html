What Is Lean Construction — and Why Iran's Projects Need It

A practical look at Lean Construction principles and how this approach can reduce waste of time, cost, and resources on Iranian construction sites.

The Roots of Lean Construction

Lean Construction is derived from the lean production philosophy pioneered in the automotive industry — an approach centered on identifying and eliminating "waste" in processes. In construction, waste isn't just discarded material; unnecessary site stoppages, rework caused by poor coordination, and waiting for drawing approvals are all forms of hidden waste on construction projects.

How It Differs from Traditional Project Management

In traditional management, planning is typically done once at the start of the project, and project control relies on comparing the plan against reality. Lean Construction, by contrast, emphasizes collaborative and continuous planning; tools like the Last Planner System let execution teams update their weekly plan based on actual site conditions, rather than a static plan drawn up on day one.

Why This Approach Matters for Iranian Projects

  • Poor coordination among subcontractors is one of the most common causes of delay on Iranian construction projects; Lean tools structure that coordination.
  • Fluctuating material prices have turned waste reduction from a "nice to have" into an economic necessity.
  • Growing competition in private-sector projects is pushing owners toward contractors who can offer more reliable delivery times.

Where to Start

Implementing Lean Construction doesn't require overhauling the entire organization overnight. The best starting point is usually a pilot run of the Last Planner System on a limited portion of the project, measuring indicators like Percent Plan Complete (PPC), and gradually expanding to other areas. This incremental path keeps the risk of change manageable while delivering early, tangible results to project management.
